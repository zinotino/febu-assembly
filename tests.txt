FOUR TESTS (for Witness to pick apart; rewritten 2026-10-14 by Z for ledger version 2, updated for 2.1 and version 3)

THE QUESTION
  Witness asked what the answer to febu would look like if we approached it the way Satoshi
  approached money, and named what Satoshi was answering: gains kept private, losses passed
  down, bailouts, and decisions made at the top. Those four are the tests. The ledger (08) is
  our answer so far. Nothing in it has been checked by anyone but Z.

THE FOUR TESTS
  1. GAINS. Who collects when it works?
     Febu:   the wallet that created $FEBU received 2,939 SOL of creator fees, 07-09 to 09-14
             (fact 6).
     Ledger: whoever asked for the work pays whoever did it, wallet to wallet. No pool, no
             treasury (rule 5). But every $FEBU trade still pays a fee to that wallet
             (KNOWN BUGS 5).
  2. LOSSES. Who pays when it fails?
     Febu:   holders. That wallet sold all its $FEBU on 09-22 and holds none (fact 2).
     Ledger: whoever breaks a promise carries BROKEN under their name for good. If the
             promise named a bond in $FEBU, the promiser sends it to the incinerator and the
             transaction is the link, and whoever owes a bond posts no new promise until it
             is paid (rule 5, Bond). Nothing holds the bond in advance. That is all. It is
             weak.
  3. BAILOUT. Can whoever runs it issue something new instead of paying for a failure?
     Febu:   not in the ledger yet. A fact about it needs a link (rule 1).
     Ledger: nothing is removed, a promise withdrawn in its last 7 days counts as BROKEN, and
             the group makes no new token (rules 2 and 5).
  4. DECISION. Is there a step only one party can perform?
     Febu:   not in the ledger yet. A fact about it needs a link (rule 1).
     Ledger: nobody approves and anyone checks. Rules, and anything done in the group's
             name, need 7 days, four yeses and at most one "object" (rule 7). Rule 6 makes a
             different member the editor each week, and the ledger is the file that matches
             the last posted hash. Not yet true for the file and the site, which are still
             Z's until each member has done a turn (KNOWN BUGS 1, fix by 2026-11-14).

BITCOIN'S THREE PROPERTIES
  1. Work that cannot be faked. PARTLY. A result counts only with what the checker saw at the
     link, and anyone who opens the link can show it was faked. But nothing makes anyone look,
     so a fake lasts until somebody does. Bitcoin turns a bad block away whether or not anyone
     is watching.
  2. Anyone can check for almost nothing. PARTLY. Yes for facts that are one link. Fact 6 is
     several links and a column of 44 numbers; fact 4 is 164 pages. Nobody has timed one.
  3. The rule itself pays, in the thing being protected. NO. Bitcoin could pay by rule because
     it made its own money. The group makes no new token and holds no money, so the only
     payments are people paying people. A rule that pays needs money held somewhere, and
     whoever holds it is test 4 again.
     Version 3's bond comes closest: by rule a broken promise costs its promiser $FEBU,
     which goes to the incinerator. But the promiser has to send it, and nobody is paid to
     look.

THE FOUNDER TEST
  Bitcoin kept working when Satoshi stepped away. Febu's writing stopped on 09-19 and never
  restarted. The ledger fails this test today: the file and the site are Z's. Version 3
  starts to change that (rule 6): a different member edits each week, and the ledger is the
  file that matches the last posted hash, wherever it is served. It still fails until a
  second member has done a turn (KNOWN BUGS 1).

$FEBU
  The group makes no new token, and $FEBU is the only token used (rule 5, proposed). Using
  $FEBU means its trades pay a fee to the wallet that created it; in the trade
  checked, that wallet's own 09-22 sale, just under 1% of the SOL (fact 6, step 1). pump.fun lets a community apply to move an abandoned
  coin's fee to a new wallet. Whether to apply, and who would hold that wallet, is a decision
  in the group's name (rule 7).
  Version 3 (2026-10-14) proposes not to apply: the creator wallet is active, and the takeover
  is run by pump.fun's own multisig (fact 9), so the fee would be held at its pleasure.
  Version 3 gives $FEBU one use: the bond a promise may name (rule 5).
  Witness's four ifs: a working system, people taking part, a role for $FEBU, demand growing
  against supply. The first three can be written as promises with dates and links. The fourth
  is a price, and nobody can promise a price.
