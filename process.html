the assembly: the whole process in one place

2026-10-15

Start here. This file says how the parts fit: the ledger, the scripts that check it, money, and the 30 days that decide whether to keep it. It adds no rule. The rules are ledger.txt (https://febu-assembly.vercel.app/ledger.txt), and where this file and the ledger differ, the ledger wins. Anything marked proposed counts only after a rule 7 vote. Three things are up for that vote now, all by 2026-10-22 (section 10).

0. The process in five lines

  1. Anyone writes a claim as an entry: what it says, a link anyone can open, and what would prove it wrong.
  2. Two members who did not write it open the link and post what they saw. The same result twice closes it.
  3. Each week one member copies the week's results into the file and posts its hash in the chat and in one named public place outside it.
  4. Nobody is paid to check and the group holds no money. A promise may name a $FEBU bond, burned by its writer if they break it.
  5. The whole thing runs as a 30-day test on the seven members, with the result that would end it written down first.

Everything below is detail on one of those five lines, or an add-on that changes none of them.

1. Why it exists

febu ran every step of a pipeline except the check. 164 pages, numbered without a gap, so nothing was ever refused; the one checker in its story, UBEF, was "removed", in the first person. Then the writing stopped (09-19) and the bag was sold (09-22).

The ledger is that missing step and nothing more. What anyone says is worth nothing until it meets a record nobody in the group controls: a transaction, an archived page, a dated file. All the weight sits at that edge, so every part of the process exists to put a claim in front of a public record and keep the check honest.

Why anyone takes part, with nobody paid: a writer gets a fact anyone can cite without trusting them, once two others close it. A promiser gets believed: a date and a bond count for more than a chat message. A checker gets something to rely on without taking anyone's word. If that is not enough for seven people, PROMISE 7 breaks and the minutes say what pay would cost.

3. The core: an entry's life

Writing it

FACT n. (who, date)                     PROMISE n. (who, date)
  SAYS      one sentence                  WILL       what they will do
  LINK      links anyone can open         BY         the date
  WRONG IF  what would show it false      KNOWN BY   the link anyone will open then
  SEEN      results, as they come         IF BROKEN  the bond in $FEBU, or none
                                          SEEN       results, on or after the date
  • Every part of SAYS needs a WRONG IF line that would catch it. If SAYS claims more, cut SAYS.
  • Every link opens for anyone, without asking the person who posted it.
  • Nobody approves an entry or judges whether it is worth having. One nobody needs goes unchecked and stays OPEN.
  • A forecast of something nobody here does ("the sun will rise tomorrow") is neither. It goes in the chat.
  • Anything the group tells outsiders about what will happen goes in first, as a promise.
  • A habit, not yet a rule: a WRONG IF line must not be something a stranger can make true. "Holds none now" and "nothing out after 09-14" can be tripped by anyone, with a dust transfer or the permissionless fee claim. End-date them: "by the end of 2026-10-14". The scripts found this; facts 2, 6 and 8 carry CHANGED lines that fix it.

Before anyone checks

  • The writer may correct SAYS or WRONG IF with a dated CHANGED line while SEEN says "nobody yet". Never after.
  • Proposed for round two: a second. Before anyone checks, someone other than the writer says they want it checked and that its WRONG IF lines test what it says. If adopted, it stays optional: a second that every entry must wait for is an approval, and nobody approves an entry.

Checking it

  1. Open the link. You need no account.
  2. Look for what the entry says is there before reading anyone else's post about it.
  3. Post: the entry number, what you saw, which WRONG IF lines you tested (for a line over many items, how many items you opened), one thing on the page nobody has written yet, and your result. A post that only says HOLDS counts for nothing. Add the minutes it took: not a rule, but E1 and any pay floor are measured from them.
  4. Your post goes under the entry as a SEEN line.

Nobody checks their own entry. An entry in the group's name has a writer: the member who proposed it. An AI may help you look; it never posts a result.

How it ends

OPEN ──two members, same result──▶ HOLDS / DOESN'T HOLD   (a fact)
  ▲                                KEPT / BROKEN          (a promise)
  │                                       │
  └──two people, member or not, post ─────┘   reopened; it closes again the same way,
     the same different result                counting only posts made after it reopened

one person disagrees       ──▶  a NOTE under the entry; the entry stays as it was
30 days without closing    ──▶  CAN'T TELL

"The same result" means the same word on the same WRONG IF lines. A line that covers many items (pages, payments, transactions) counts toward HOLDS only if tested over all of them: every page, every payment, or the explorer's whole filtered list. A sample stays a SEEN line; one that finds a wrong item counts toward DOESN'T HOLD. Nothing is removed. A wrong fact stays, marked DOESN'T HOLD, under its writer's name: that is a fact's whole cost. A fact never carries a bond.

Promises

  • A promise can be withdrawn up to 7 days before its date. Withdrawn later, it counts as BROKEN.
  • The bond is optional. The promiser picks the amount in $FEBU, with no minimum, and a reader weighs the promise by it.
  • On BROKEN: within 7 days the promiser sends the bond to the incinerator or burns it, and the transaction is the link. It is burned, not paid to the person promised, who would then have a reason to want BROKEN. Unpaid, the entry says UNPAID. Whoever owes a bond posts no new promise until it is paid. No contract holds bonds in advance, so that is all the enforcement there is.
  • A promise to pay names the amount, what it is paid in, and the date. The payment itself is a fact.

A member silent in the chat for 90 days stops counting for rules 3, 6 and 7 until they post again. Witness is one of the seven, with one vote: not a gate, and nothing waits on Witness. Results are posted in the chat and added to the file by the weekly turn; no page writes into the ledger, because a typed name would let one person act as seven. Anyone may reopen an entry with a second person, but only members can post in the chat, so a non-member has nowhere to do it yet (KNOWN BUGS 7). The fix is an issue on the ledger's public repository, which the weekly turn adds to the file.

The weekly turn (rule 6)

Each week the next member in the list order starts from the file whose hash was last posted, checks both ways (every result posted in the chat that week is in the file; every SEEN line in the file has its chat post), adds what is missing, and posts the new file's SHA-256 in the chat and in one named public place outside it; the chat post sets the order. A turn not done by the end of its week passes on. The ledger is whichever copy matches the last posted hash, wherever it is served.

The outside record

Four things nobody in the group controls: the GitHub history, the hash posted outside the chat, a daily Internet Archive capture, and an OpenTimestamps proof in a Bitcoin block. None of them stops an edit. Together they make every edit visible, with a date nobody in the group set. Bitcoin is used for that stamp and nothing else.

Rules and bugs (rules 7 and 8)

  • A rule changes, and anything is done in the group's name, only after 7 days in the chat, four yeses from active members (every active member, if fewer than five are active) and at most one "object". The old wording is archived with its hash first.
  • An objection says what would change the objector's mind. A habit for now, proposed as a rule in round two.
  • Anything only one person can do is a bug, listed under KNOWN BUGS with a fix-by date. Bugs 1 to 3 and 7 are due 2026-11-14; 4 is fixed on the site; 5 has a proposed close (section 6); 6 is the name claim's codes and key, held by Z (the claim is gone from the site; the ledger's line is closed after the version 3 vote); 7 is that a non-member has nowhere to reopen an entry.
  • Anyone may copy the file and run it their own way.

5. Add-on A: scripts

A script does the reading for a claim a public record settles. Writing one is work; running one is one command. That gap is the whole reason to have them.

Where they fit. Five of the nine entries settle this way (1, 2, 6, 8, 9); fact 3 half; facts 4 and 5 run, but on Z's copy of the scripture (KNOWN BUGS 3), and say so in their first words; they cover every page, so under rule 3 they close only by reading all 164 pages by hand or, if the Saw line passes, by a script run; PROMISE 7 is people. The entries were picked because they could be checked, so five of nine is a ceiling, not an average.

What makes a script signable:

  1. It reads the addresses from the ledger's own text, not from values typed into it.
  2. It opens with an ASSUMES block in plain words, one line per assumption.
  3. It is tried on controls first: one copy of the real record per WRONG IF line, each with one thing changed, each of which it must call wrong. Anyone can make these on the spot, and the script's writer cannot see them in advance.
  4. Its output says which WRONG IF lines tripped.

The person signs the script, its ASSUMES block and the controls' output. A script without ASSUMES is not signable. Running catches loud failures; only a control catches a wrong assumption, which is why ASSUMES comes first.

What running them taught:

  • Refuting is cheap: one witness does it. Confirming that nothing happened is expensive unless the flow has a quiet end. Fact 8 read 3 transactions where the obvious way needed 5,226.
  • A script reads what is written, not what was meant. Fact 8's "at any time on 09-22" was false as worded; 00:00 UTC was meant.
  • The fee claim is permissionless, and a second call anyone can send skips the account fact 8 reads. Fact 8 needs both reads, resolve.py fact8 and resolve.py door2.
  • Never send the fee claim to see what happens. It pays the dev wallet and makes a WRONG IF line true.

What it needs from the rules: one line, proposed under rule 7, in WORDS USED HERE:

Saw: you opened the link yourself, or you ran the entry's script yourself and opened one link by hand. An AI may help you find something on the page or write the script. It never posts a result.

Without it, scripts still help people look, but a script run cannot close an entry, and facts 4 and 5 (every page) close only by someone reading all 164 pages by hand. With it, two closers may run the same script, and then its mistakes are made twice. What keeps them two checks is the link each opens by hand and the one new thing each names; E3 (section 7) tests whether that is enough.

Scripts live in checks/ in the ledger's repository, https://github.com/zinotino/febu-assembly/tree/main/checks. They rest on one free node that keeps history (api.mainnet-beta.solana.com); until there is a second, the explorer link opened by hand is the second source.

6. Money: all of it

In the core

  • The group holds nothing. No pool, no vault, no treasury, no new token. Money moves wallet to wallet, as promises kept.
  • The only money in the core is bonds, burned by whoever broke the promise.
  • No price. No entry and no post in the group's name states or implies a price for $FEBU, "demand" and "supply" included.
  • $FEBU's catch (KNOWN BUGS 5). Every $FEBU trade pays a fee to the wallet that created it and sold out, including a buy made to post a bond. Any post asking people to trade $FEBU says where the fee goes. Proposed close: the group does not apply to take over the fee (pump.fun's own multisig runs takeovers, fact 9). Decided when version 3 is adopted.

The dev wallet, ApWpkd8

The subject, never a purse.

  • The subject. Every money claim febu made is a claim about it. It is also febu's last unforgeable identity: a signature from it over an entry's hash would be the one answer from febu a stranger can verify. That invitation can stand; nothing is sent to the wallet, not even a message.
  • Not a purse. Nobody claims its fee: that would make the group a treasury and febu's heir.
  • Not a recipient. A dust transfer can trip a WRONG IF line; fact 2's could, before its fix.
  • Not a counterparty. Nothing pays out on what it does next, because whoever can move it would win. Anyone who can move it says so in one dated line.

A member who pays for work

Exists only as that member's own promise, with a bond, closed under rule 3 like any other. The group still pays nobody, and nothing opens before the stop rule's dated line says keep. The shape, its cap and its own two tests are in paid.md, beside this file, for whoever offers. It is not part of the vote.

What it is worth

The group does not decide. It makes checking cheap and leaves the price to whoever wants the answer, as Bitcoin's rules fix the cost of a check and not the coin's worth.

  • A check: its cost in minutes is measured in the unpaid weeks (E1). That is the floor for any pay: below it nobody checks, and far above it people check what cannot be false.
  • A task: a member's own promise, with a bond, naming the pay for a script two others reproduce. Anyone may post a competing one. If nobody takes the work, it was worth less than the offer to everyone else.
  • Creative work: no record settles whether it is good. The ledger shows that it was published, when, and that its author kept their promise. The audience prices the rest.
  • The self-sustaining loop is unproven. It closes only if people other than the funder post bounties, which is E5. Until day 30 nobody is paid; the only pay that can open after it is the funder's, for scripts.

E3's remedy is the old way of checking. A miss all three drafts share is caught here by a copy, but it predicts the misses no copy covers, and two closers whose scripts share one would close a false fact. A person reading ASSUMES might share the miss and leaves no record; opening every link by hand leaves the one new thing on the page.

8. Lines that never move

  • Nothing is removed. Every change is a new dated line. Only personal information may become "[removed]".
  • Nobody checks their own entry.
  • An AI never posts a result.
  • No treasury, no new token, no price.
  • No totals, no rankings, no scores. What stands under a name is what happened, entry by entry.
  • Nothing is sent to the dev wallet, its fee is not claimed, and no fee claim is sent as a test.
  • Nobody tries to identify private individuals. Chain tracing stops at the payee.
  • Full wallet addresses appear only in ledger.txt and ledger.html.

9. What it cannot do
  • Hold a claim with no public record. Three of febu's problems (repeating history, machines that imitate, human arrogance) have none yet: that is the ledger's OPEN QUESTION.
  • Settle what a link means. It settles what a link shows; interpretation stays in the chat.
  • Tell one person from two names, or a real check from an AI-written one a person signed without looking, beyond the "one new thing" rule.
  • Tell two runs of one script from two checks, beyond the link each closer opens by hand. E3 measures how much that matters.
  • Beat two people who agree in advance. They can close a false fact, or hold an entry open, until someone else looks.
  • Keep out easy entries. One anyone could get right without looking can be posted and closed; it counts toward PROMISE 7 and shows nothing, and it stays under its writer's name for anyone to read. The full list is RISKS WE ACCEPT.
  • Make anyone look, or pay anyone to. If the seven do not check, PROMISE 7 breaks, and that is the honest result.
  • Stop an edit. It makes one visible.
  • Escape the multipolar trap. Scripts make one kind of cheating cost more, and that is all.
