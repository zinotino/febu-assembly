ONE SENTENCE

A claim costs a link and a line saying what would prove it wrong; a check costs a look by two people who did not write it; the result goes in one text file that anyone can copy and nobody can quietly edit.

WHERE IT STANDS

Version 3 of the ledger is proposed, not adopted, and nobody but its writer has checked anything in it. Until 2026-10-22 the seven members post "yes" or "object" in the chat on three things: the ledger as served, the Saw line (running an entry's script, then opening one link by hand, may count as looking), and the stop rule (the 30-day test, E0 to E3). The same week is a dry run: facts 1, 3 and 9 are the cheapest to check, and a post says how many minutes it took. Posts made before adoption do not count toward PROMISE 7. The 30 days start at adoption.

HOW IT CONNECTS

Five places, left to right. The record outside the group is what a claim is about: a transaction, an archived page, a dated file. A writer turns it into an entry and posts it in the chat. Two members who did not write it open its link and post what they saw; if the Saw line passes, they may instead run its script and open one link by hand. Members vote on a rule change. Once a week one member makes sure every result posted in the chat is in the file and every SEEN line has its chat post, then posts the file's hash in the chat and in one named public place. Two matching results close an entry. For PROMISE 7 a close counts on the day of its second post; the file catches up at the next weekly turn. Anyone may add a SEEN line to the file. The site is built from the texts and adds no rule: where it and ledger.txt differ, ledger.txt wins.

THE FOUR PARTS

1.

The loop (rules 1 to 4). An entry is a FACT or a PROMISE. A fact says what is true now, gives a link anyone can open, and carries a WRONG IF line, fixed before any check, saying what would show it false. A promise says what its writer will do, by when, and the link anyone will open then; it may name a bond. Two members who did not write the entry open the link and post what they saw and one thing on the page nobody had written; a post that only says HOLDS counts for nothing. The same result twice closes it. One person's disagreement is a note under it. Two people, member or not, who post the same different result reopen it (a non-member has nowhere to post it yet: KNOWN BUGS 7). 30 days without a close is CAN'T TELL. A line over many items (pages, payments) counts toward HOLDS only if tested over all of them. The writer may correct an entry with a dated CHANGED line until its first SEEN line, never after. Nobody approves an entry and nobody checks their own. An AI may help someone look; it never posts a result.

2.

The keeping (rule 6). One file, ledger.txt. Each week the next member in the list checks both ways, adds what is missing, and posts the new file's SHA-256 in the chat and in one named public place outside it. The ledger is whichever copy matches the last posted hash. Three records sit outside the group: that posted hash, a daily request to the Internet Archive to save ledger.txt, and a Bitcoin timestamp (OpenTimestamps), which is not made yet. GitHub holds the history too, but the repository is Z's (KNOWN BUGS 1) and its history was cut once, on 2026-10-15, when the ledger started clean. None of these stops an edit. Together they make an edit visible, with a date no member set.

3.

The changing (rules 7 and 8). A rule changes, and anything is done in the group's name, only after 7 days in the chat, four yeses from active members (every active member, if fewer than five are active) and at most one "object". The old wording stays. Anything only one person can do is a bug, listed under KNOWN BUGS with a date to fix it by. Today that includes: Z holds the file, the site and the name-claim codes. A member silent for 90 days stops counting until they post again.

4.

The test (PROMISE 7 and the stop rule). Thirty days after adoption, unpaid, on the seven. The group's goal, PROMISE 7: close three entries besides itself, at least one not written by Z. Miss it and it is BROKEN: we say so, then rewrite or stop. The stop rule adds four tests, E0 to E3, each with its failing number written first and what happens when it fails. The first honest numbers on what a check costs in minutes come out of this part.

Money is not a part. The group holds none: no pool, no treasury, no new token. A promise may name a bond in $FEBU; if it ends BROKEN its writer sends the bond to the incinerator and the transaction is the link. A fact never carries a bond. The group pays nobody; a member may pay another only by their own promise.

WHAT IT RUNS ON

GitHub for the history (Z's repository), Vercel for the pages and a few small functions, a small free database for name claims, a public Solana RPC and explorer links for chain facts, web.archive.org for the daily capture, and the group chat for posts. It runs on free tiers. If Vercel or GitHub vanish, the file survives in every copy anyone made. If the Internet Archive vanishes, a Bitcoin timestamp would still show the file existed (none is made yet). If the chat vanishes, the posts move; the ledger does not.

Bitcoin is planned for one thing: a timestamp no member could have forged. Not for money, not for agreement, not for identity. That is the whole lean.

WHAT IT CANNOT DO

-

Hold a claim with no public record. "Febu is honest" cannot be an entry. "The fee account paid nothing out from 09-15 to the end of 10-14" can. A claim a stranger could make false (a dust transfer, the fee claim anyone may send) needs an end date; facts 2, 6 and 8 were changed for that.

-

Settle what a link means. It settles what a link shows. Interpretation stays in the chat.

-

Work among strangers. It cannot tell one person from two names, or a real check from a copied one beyond the "one new thing" rule.

-

Make anyone look. The group pays nobody. If the seven do not check, PROMISE 7 breaks, and that is the honest result. The dry run's minutes will say what a check costs.

-

Stop an edit. Anyone with the repository can edit; the edit cannot be hidden. A capture and a stamp prove what the file was, not that the file was right.

-

Bind anyone. No entity, no contract, no legal weight. A bond is kept by the rule and by shame, not by code.

-

Detect a machine. A check written by a model and posted by a person is undetectable; the AI line is a rule, not a filter.

-

Show that anyone outside the seven wants it. Seven people and 30 days will say about the seven, not about outsiders.

HOW IT ANSWERS FEBU

This section is Z's reading of febu's pages and tweets. None of it is an entry yet, except the 09-22 sale (FACT 2).

Febu's pages name eleven problems: corrupt leaders, invented debt, repeating history, arrogance, war, dependence on machines, machines that imitate, hidden systems, hollow law, misinformation, the environment. Read together they share one disease: speech that costs nothing. In every case the sentence was free to say and nobody had to check it.

Febu is the purest case of its own disease. 164 pages, every one about what is wrong, none about how anything should work, none with a claim you could test. Written by a script, on a schedule, numbered without a gap: no sign anything was ever refused. The one checker the story had, UBEF, was "removed", in the first person. "Every page survives an argument" was said five minutes after being asked who UBEF was. Then the writing stopped (09-19) and the bag was sold (09-22).

So the ledger is not a fan project and not a treasury. It is the one step febu's pipeline never ran: the check.

FOUR QUESTIONS FROM THE CHAT

WitnesstoFebu read it as: a claim with a link, a WRONG IF and a bond; a cheap check; a result set by what the link shows; a record that builds up. Right, except the bond: only a promise can carry one. A fact never does. A wrong fact costs its writer the DOESN'T HOLD line.

1.

How big is a bond? The promiser picks it, in $FEBU, with no minimum. A reader weighs the promise by it: a promise backed by 10 $FEBU says less than one backed by a million. The group never says what that is worth in money (rule 5).

2.

Does a smart contract burn it? No. Within 7 days of a promise closing BROKEN, the promiser sends the bond to the incinerator, or burns it, and the transaction is the link. A contract holding bonds in advance would be a pool, which rule 5 forbids, and code someone has to write and run is a step only that person can do, which rule 8 lists as a bug. Unpaid after 7 days, the entry says UNPAID, and its owner posts no new promise until it is paid. That is all the enforcement there is.

3.

Why take part? The group pays nobody, and reputation here is a list, not a score: nothing is summed. A writer gets a fact anyone can cite without trusting them, once two others close it. A promiser gets believed: a promise with a date and a bond counts for more than a chat message. A checker gets something to rely on without taking anyone's word. If seven people will not check unpaid, PROMISE 7 breaks, and the dry run's minutes say what a check costs.

4.

Can I enter "the sun will rise tomorrow"? No. It is a forecast of something nobody here does, so it is neither a fact (true now, and a link shows it) nor a promise (something you will do). An easy fact that fits ("febu's site is up") can be entered: nobody approves or refuses anything. It costs two checkers a minute and earns nothing, because nothing is added up. Usefulness is decided by whether anyone bothers to check: an entry nobody needs stays OPEN. An easy entry could still count toward PROMISE 7 (RISKS WE ACCEPT).

WHAT IT COULD BECOME

-

A dated record of what febu said and did, built from entries, that a holder can check without trusting the Assembly.

-

A template. The file and the rules copy in a minute; any small group whose members know each other can run its own.

-

A bond behind a promise, in $FEBU: a use that is not trading. Buying $FEBU for one still pays its fee to the wallet that created it (KNOWN BUGS 5).

-

If febu returns, a member can give each new page a day-one entry: what it claims, what would show it wrong.

-

Promise records without a scoreboard: KEPT and BROKEN lines with links, nothing summed.

-

The first real numbers on what a check costs in minutes, from the cheapest entries first.
