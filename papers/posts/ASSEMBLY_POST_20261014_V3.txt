3. What version 3 changes.
- Rule 3: a disagreement or a reopening needs two people, the same bar as closing. One person's disagreement
  is kept as a NOTE.
- Rule 6: the ledger is whichever file matches the last posted hash, and the weekly member makes the week's
  changes. The file stops being mine.
- Rule 7 and members: anyone silent for 90 days stops counting until they post again. Otherwise four quiet
  members could freeze every change for good.
- Checking: a result names one thing on the page that nobody has written yet. A copied result looked like a
  check before.
- Rule 5: a promise can name a bond in $FEBU. If the promise is BROKEN, the promiser sends the bond to the
  incinerator, where nobody can spend it, and the transaction is the link. Nobody who owes a bond posts a new
  promise. And no entry or post in the group's name says or implies a price for $FEBU.
- PROMISE 7 is the group's own failure test: three entries closed within 30 days of adoption, at least one not
  mine. If BROKEN, we say so and rewrite or stop.
- KNOWN BUGS 5 gets a proposed close: we do not apply for the fee, and any post asking anyone to trade $FEBU
  says where the fee goes.

4. The money answer, for Witness.
The Satoshi move was: make a thing cost real work, make checking it cost nothing, and let the rule pay
instead of a person. Febu's eleven problems share one disease, speech that costs nothing, and febu is the
purest case of it. So the Satoshi model applied to febu is proof of work for claims. A claim costs a WRONG IF
line, a link and a bond. A check costs a click. A broken promise burns its bond by rule. $FEBU's role is the
bond: a broken promise's bond goes to the incinerator by rule, and the group holds nothing and
pays nobody. That passes your four tests more cleanly than any vault did.
What it does not solve: the multipolar trap, which Bitcoin did not solve either, and paying anyone to look.
Thirty days will tell whether seven named people look unpaid. If they do not, a reward layer is back on the
table with real numbers (Ksizzle's shape, flat pay and a loss on a wrong vote, held in the simulation only
when a wrong vote cost two and outside money came in).
