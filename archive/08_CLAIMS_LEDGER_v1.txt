CLAIMS LEDGER (starter, drafted 2026-10-12 by Z)

WHAT THIS IS
  A list of claims about febu. Each claim names a record anyone can open, and says what would
  prove it wrong before anyone checks it. Two other people check it. The result is posted
  either way, and a wrong claim stays on the list.

THREE JOBS. NOBODY HOLDS TWO OF THEM ON THE SAME CLAIM.
  Propose   writes the claim, the source, and what would prove it wrong.
  Record    keeps this list and copies each verdict in. Never edits a claim or a verdict.
  Check     opens the source and posts RIGHT, WRONG or CAN'T TELL, with one line saying why.
            A CAN'T TELL says what was opened and what was missing.

  Z should not be the record keeper: most of the first claims are Z's.

RULES
   1. Nobody checks their own claim.
   2. Nobody picks their checker, and nobody can know who it will be before the claim is
      posted. The seats are drawn from a Bitcoin block that did not exist yet when the claim
      went up. See THE DRAW.                                          (changed 2026-10-13)
   3. The source must open without asking the proposer: a public archive link, a chain explorer
      page, or a page number in a text the checker already holds. "It is in my database" does
      not count.
   4. "Wrong if" is written before the check and is not changed afterwards.
   5. Verdicts are sealed until all are in. Each checker first posts a lock in the group
      chat, and opens it only when every checker on that claim has posted one. See SEALED
      VERDICTS. The opened verdict, posted as the checker's own message, is the record.
      This list is a copy of it.                                      (changed 2026-10-13)
   6. Two matching verdicts from the two checkers give the result. If the two disagree, the
      tie-breaker from the draw decides. While the group has fewer than 20 members, two
      re-checkers also check every claim, and it closes only if neither of them says the
      opposite. See RE-CHECK.                                         (changed 2026-10-13)
   7. A claim sent back three times goes to the whole group.
   8. A WRONG stays on the list under its author's name. Corrections go underneath it.
   9. An AI may fetch a source and lay it out for a checker. It does not give the verdict.
      An AI may also post an objection to an open claim, quoting the line of the source it
      rests on. If the result comes out the opposite of a standing objection, the claim
      goes to the whole group. An AI can send a claim to people. It can never close one.
      The member who posts the objection answers for it.              (changed 2026-10-13)
  10. Optional: the record keeper may add up to two claims they know to be false. Everyone
      knows this can happen and nobody knows which ones. If both checkers mark a planted claim
      RIGHT, the checking is not working. A CAN'T TELL on a planted claim is a miss too.
  11. Being drawn is earned, and checking is by choice. See MEMBERS.
  12. Nothing that closed can be dropped or reordered without it showing. See THE RECORD.
  13. A rule changes the way a claim is made: posted, waited on, and the old one kept. See
      CHANGING THE RULES.
  14. This list never adds anyone up. No totals, no rankings, no scores, and nothing here
      depends on one. What stands under a name is what happened, entry by entry.
                                                            (11 to 14 added 2026-10-13)

MEMBERS (rules 2 and 11)
  Anyone can read this list, propose a claim and post a promise. Being drawn to check is
  narrower, so that nobody can fill the draw with new names just before a claim.
  1. Founding members are everyone the group names in one chat message on the day it
     starts using this list. That message is the first member list.
  2. After that, a person can be drawn once two things are true: a claim of their own has
     closed RIGHT, and 30 days have passed since they asked in the chat to join.
  3. Checking is by choice. A member is in the pool from when they say "in" in the chat
     until they say "out". Nobody is marked down for staying out.
  4. The pool for a claim is the pool as it stood when the claim was posted. The record
     keeper keeps the current pool posted in the chat.
  5. The whole group names the record keeper and can name a new one. The record keeper is
     never in the pool.

THE DRAW (rule 2)
  When   Take the time the group chat shows on the claim's message, in UTC, and add two
         hours. The draw block is the first Bitcoin block whose time is at or past that.
         Any block explorer shows each block's height, time and hash.
         Why two hours: the Bitcoin network refuses a block dated more than two hours
         ahead. A block dated that late cannot have existed when the claim was posted, so
         nobody could have known its hash, the proposer included.
  Who    Everyone in the pool when the claim was posted (see MEMBERS), in A to Z order,
         leaving out the proposer. Number them from 0.
  How    Take the last 12 characters of the block's hash. They are a number written in
         base 16. Turn it into an ordinary number (search "hex to decimal").
         Divide it by how many names are on the list. The remainder points at a name, and
         0 is the first. That name takes the next seat and comes off the list. Keep the
         whole-number part of the division, and do the same again with the shorter list.
         The seats, in order: checker 1, checker 2, re-checker 1, re-checker 2, tie-breaker.
         Names drawn after that are spares, in order.
  Check  Whoever does the draw posts the block height, the hash and the order. It counts
         once a second member has redone it and got the same order. draw.py, in the same
         folder as this list, does the arithmetic. So does a calculator.
  Fixed  A claim keeps its first draw. Posting it again, in the same words or new ones,
         does not get a new one. A checker who has posted nothing 7 days after the draw
         loses the seat, the names after it each move up one, and the missed check is noted
         on this list. If there are too few members to fill the four checking seats, the
         claim waits.

  EXAMPLE (made-up members, a real block)
    Members: Ada, Ben, Cal, Dot, Eli, Fay, Gus. Gus keeps the record. Ada posts a claim at
    2026-10-12 15:00 UTC. Two hours later is 17:00. The first block dated at or past that
    is block 971,119, dated 17:00:41 UTC. Its hash is
      000000000000000000009be34d563b691ec3f0b2935e88ec56ceace95b11755c
    The last 12 characters, ace95b11755c, are the number 190,118,255,228,252.
    The list is 0 Ben, 1 Cal, 2 Dot, 3 Eli, 4 Fay.
      190,118,255,228,252 / 5 = 38,023,651,045,650  remainder 2   Dot is checker 1
       38,023,651,045,650 / 4 =  9,505,912,761,412  remainder 2   Eli is checker 2
        9,505,912,761,412 / 3 =  3,168,637,587,137  remainder 1   Cal is re-checker 1
        3,168,637,587,137 / 2 =  1,584,318,793,568  remainder 1   Fay is re-checker 2
                                                                  Ben is the tie-breaker
    (After Dot comes off, the list is 0 Ben, 1 Cal, 2 Eli, 3 Fay, and so on.)

SEALED VERDICTS (rule 5)
  A checker who can see another's verdict can copy it. So nobody shows theirs first.
  1. Write one line: the verdict as one word, one space, and a private word of at least 8
     letters or digits that nobody could guess. Nothing after it, not even a space.
     The verdict words are RIGHT, WRONG and CANTTELL (for CAN'T TELL), or KEPT and BROKEN
     for a promise.
       Example:  WRONG walnut7731
  2. Put that exact line through SHA-256 (search "sha256 online", or run draw.py lock) and
     post the result in the chat. That is the lock. It shows you have decided without
     showing what. The lock for the example line is
       fd548234041d9e55167c3109916ff23e5b3210a94d6cdc13ea7325c883377cb5
  3. When every checker and re-checker on the claim has posted a lock, each one posts their
     line and one sentence saying why. Anyone can put the line through SHA-256 and see that
     it gives the lock.
  4. A line that does not give its lock is not counted. That checker loses the seat and
     the names after it each move up one.
  5. A lock that is never opened is listed as LOCKED, NOT OPENED under that checker's
     name. Whoever takes the seat has already seen the other verdicts, so theirs is
     marked as not blind.
  draw.py lock makes the private word for you, so nobody picks one that can be guessed.
  If you cannot make a lock: send your line privately to the record keeper before any
  verdict on that claim is opened. The record keeper posts "held for" and your name, and
  nothing else, then posts your line when the others open theirs.

RE-CHECK (rule 6, while the group has fewer than 20 members)
  With about seven members, three who agreed in advance could pass a false claim: one
  writes it, and the other two are drawn as its checkers about 1 time in 10. So for now:
  1. The two re-checkers check every claim, at the same time as the checkers and sealed
     the same way.
  2. If either re-checker's verdict is the opposite of the result (RIGHT against WRONG,
     KEPT against BROKEN), the claim does not close. It goes to the whole group.
  3. CAN'T TELL from a re-checker does not stop a claim closing.
  4. If the whole group overturns a checker or re-checker, that is noted under their name
     on this list, the same way a WRONG is under an author's (rule 8).
  When the group reaches 20 members it decides whether to keep this.

THE WHOLE GROUP (rules 6 and 7)
  A claim sent to the whole group is checked by every member except the proposer and the
  record keeper. Each may post a verdict within 7 days, sealed the same way.
  1. It closes on RIGHT or WRONG (KEPT or BROKEN) if at least four members post and at
     least two-thirds of those who post agree.
  2. Otherwise it closes as CAN'T TELL, and the list says the group could not settle it.
  3. This is the last step. There is nobody above it.
  This step only works if most members are honest. Nothing in this list can fix a group
  where they are not. Bitcoin rests on the same thing.

THE RECORD (rule 12)
  The chat messages are the record (rule 5). This part guards the list of what closed, so
  that nothing can be dropped or reordered later without it showing.
  1. When a claim or promise closes, the record keeper adds one line to the chain, a plain
     list kept next to this one (CHAIN.txt):
       line number | entry | result | date closed, UTC | the SHA-256 of the line before
     The first line has the word start where the earlier hash would go.
  2. The record keeper posts the new line and its SHA-256 in the chat. Anyone with a copy
     can work out every hash again. A line that is changed, removed or moved breaks every
     hash after it.
  3. In any week when something closed, the newest hash is also posted somewhere public
     that the group does not run. An X post is enough. That fixes a date nobody in the
     group can move.
  4. Anyone may keep a copy of the chain and of this list. If two copies disagree, the one
     whose hashes match the public posts is right.
  draw.py close adds a line and draw.py chain checks a copy. Any "sha256 online" page does
  the same by hand.
  What this does not guard is the wording of a claim. For that the chat message is still
  the record.

  EXAMPLE (made-up entries)
    1 | example-A | RIGHT | 2026-11-02 | start
        its SHA-256: f3e91ada72d5de2777b5bacd52c08382f3efa5cd21dc8a5308b6fdf7a6d03ccb
    2 | example-B | WRONG | 2026-11-05 | f3e91ada72d5de2777b5bacd52c08382f3efa5cd21dc8a5308b6fdf7a6d03ccb
        its SHA-256: 867359db3e16470c6f67a685868c501ed3d1f3da0567320870c93d9d4cacc3f8

CHANGING THE RULES (rule 13)
  Until the first claim closes these rules are a draft. Z wrote them and changed them
  alone, which is the thing they are meant to end. From the first closed claim on:
  1. A rule change is posted in the chat as its own message: the old wording, the new
     wording, and the weak point it answers.
  2. It waits 7 days. If no member objects in the chat, it takes effect. If anyone objects,
     it goes to the whole group.
  3. The old wording is kept under CHANGES TO THE RULES, with the date.
  4. A claim is checked under the rules in force when it was posted. A change never
     reaches back.
  5. Anyone who disagrees can copy this list and run it differently. A copy is not a
     betrayal. It is how the rules stay honest.

CITING AN ENTRY
  Anything the group or a member publishes may cite an entry by its number and result,
  for example "claims ledger entry 6, RIGHT, checked by four". An entry that has not
  closed is cited as "not yet checked". That is what this list is for: work that points
  at it can be checked by a stranger.

WHEN THE PROCESS ITSELF HAS FAILED (review 30 days after the first entry)
   - fewer than 10 claims have been closed by two checkers, or
   - any claim was closed by its own author, or
   - a planted false claim was marked RIGHT by both checkers.
   - a promise (see below) is more than 14 days past its date with no result posted, or
   - any claim was closed without a draw that two members agreed on, or without its re-check, or
   - a copy of the chain does not match the hashes posted in public, or
   - a rule was changed without the 7-day wait.
  If any of these is true, we say so in the group and change the rules or stop.

ENTRY FORMAT
  No. | Claim | Source | Wrong if | Proposer | Checker 1 | Checker 2 | Result


PROMISES: A SECOND KIND OF ENTRY (added 2026-10-13)
  A claim says what happened. A promise says what someone will do. Febu's promises were
  announcements: nothing was set aside and no record said whether they were kept. Here a
  promise is written down before the work starts and checked the same way a claim is.

  P-No. | Promise | By when | Kept if | Set aside | Promiser | Checker 1 | Checker 2 | Result

  P1. If the group, or a member speaking for it, tells people outside the group that
      something will happen, it goes on this list first.
  P2. "Kept if" names a record anyone can open (rule 3) and is written with the promise.
      It is not changed afterwards (rule 4).
  P3. Every promise has a date. The draw for a promise is made when that date ends (midnight
      UTC plus the two hours), not when the promise was posted, so nobody knows the
      checkers while the work is going on. They post KEPT, BROKEN or CAN'T TELL, sealed and
      re-checked the same way as a claim.
  P4. "Set aside" says what the promiser loses if the promise is broken. To start with it
      is only this: BROKEN stays on the list under their name (rule 8). If money is ever set
      aside, it sits where the promiser cannot take it back alone, and this list says where.
  P5. A promise can be withdrawn before its date. WITHDRAWN stays on the list. Nothing is
      deleted. A promise that failed is shown as failed.

CHANGES TO THE RULES (the old wording is kept here)
  2026-10-13. Rules 2, 5 and 6 changed before any claim was checked. Three weak points in
  the first draft: the draw could be steered, the second checker could copy the first, and
  three members agreeing in advance could pass a false claim.
    Old rule 2: "Nobody picks their checker. Members are listed A to Z. For claim number N,
                count N names down the list, going round. That person and the next one check
                it. Skip the proposer."
                Weak point: a proposer could see who would check a claim before posting it,
                and time the post.
    Old rule 5: "Each checker posts the verdict as their own message in the group chat. That
                message is the record. This list is a copy of it."
                Weak point: the second checker could read the first verdict before deciding.
    Old rule 6: "Two matching verdicts close a claim. If the two disagree, a third member
                decides."
                Weak point: nothing looked at a claim again once two checkers agreed.
  2026-10-13, second set, still before any claim was checked. Rules 9 and 10 extended.
  Rules 11 to 14 added, with MEMBERS, THE WHOLE GROUP, THE RECORD, CHANGING THE RULES and
  CITING AN ENTRY. THE DRAW now draws from the pool and not from every member. The Check
  job now says what a CAN'T TELL has to contain. SEALED VERDICTS gained point 5.
    Old rule 9:  "An AI may fetch a source and lay it out for a checker. It does not give
                 the verdict."
    Old rule 10 ended at "the checking is not working."
    Old DRAW "Who": "Everyone on the member list as it stood when the claim was posted, in
                 A to Z order, leaving out the proposer and the record keeper."
    Weak points answered: nothing said who could be drawn or who decided; one person could
    change the rules; a closed entry could be dropped from the list; a drawn member had no
    way to stay out; histories under names invited a scoreboard; "the whole group" was not
    defined; a lazy CAN'T TELL cost nothing; an AI had no way to raise a doubt.
  Still open: the founding member list and the record keeper have not been named; whether
  and how a member can be removed; the fallback in SEALED VERDICTS, which trusts the
  record keeper not to pass a held verdict on; and what gives anyone a reason to check,
  beyond their own work being citable.
