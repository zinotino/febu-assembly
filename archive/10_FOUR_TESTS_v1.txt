FOUR TESTS AND SIX STEPS (drafted 2026-10-13 by Z, for Witness to pick apart)

WHY THIS PAGE
  Witness asked: if we took the problems febu points at and approached them the way Satoshi
  approached money, what would the answer look like? He named what Satoshi was answering:
  gains kept private, losses passed down, bailouts, and decisions made at the top.
  This page turns those four into tests, runs febu through them, and marks where our own
  process stands. Nothing here has been checked by anyone but Z.

THE FOUR TESTS, AND FEBU'S ANSWERS
  [A] = anyone can open the record.  [B] = only from Z's saved copies.

  1. GAINS. Who collects when it works?
     The wallet that created $FEBU. Every trade on the main pool sets aside a fee (just under
     1% in the trade checked) that only that wallet can collect. It collected 2,939 SOL
     between 07-09 and 09-14, and 11 SOL is waiting now.                 [A, ledger entry 6]
  2. LOSSES. Who pays when it fails?
     Holders. That wallet sold its first allocation, 6.667% of supply, 116 minutes after the
     coin was created. It sold its rebuilt stake on 09-22.               [A, ledger entry 2]
  3. BAILOUT. When something fails, can the insider issue something new instead of paying?
     Yes. Announcements cost nothing: "i am now the largest holder of $febu" and "i have more
     skin in the experiment than any of you" on 08-14, sold on 09-22. The same wallet made a
     second coin, benchcoin, on 09-03.                                                   [A]
  4. DECISION. Is there a step only one party can perform?
     Yes. One admin account approved every job on record. Jobs that failed were removed
     from the list and not marked failed. Job #008 was paid 21 minutes before its approval
     was recorded.                                          [B; the payment itself is A]
  In fairness: the payouts were real. The chain shows payments to outside wallets, including
  102 SOL for job #008. What was missing was any check that came before the money.

WHAT THE TESTS SAY ABOUT OUR OWN PLANS
  - Anything that raises trading in $FEBU pays the wallet that sold out. That fails test 1.
  - The ledger passes tests 1 and 4 on paper: nobody checks their own claim and nobody picks
    their checker. It has not been run. Every entry so far is Z's and none is checked.
  - pump.fun will move a coin's creator fee to a new wallet when a community applies (its
    community-takeover process). For $FEBU that would turn the fee from a leak into money
    for jobs and checking. It would also make the group the one that gains from $FEBU
    trading, which is test 1 again unless the money can only leave by rule. Not decided,
    and not Z's to decide.

WEAK POINTS WE ALREADY KNOW
  1. The draw could be steered. The first rule 2 counted down a fixed list, so a proposer
     could see the checkers before posting. Changed 10-13: the seats are drawn from a
     Bitcoin block that did not exist when the claim was posted. Not yet used.
  2. The second checker could copy the first. Changed 10-13: verdicts are locked first and
     opened together. The locks are fiddly, and the fallback trusts the record keeper.
  3. We are about seven. If three agree in advance, one writes a false claim and the other
     two are drawn as its checkers about 1 time in 10. Changed 10-13: two more members
     re-check every claim, which stops a group of three. The cost is that four of the five
     members who can check have to look at every claim.
  4. Nothing said who could be drawn or who decided. Changed 10-13: being drawn is earned
     (one claim of your own closed RIGHT, and 30 days), checking is by choice, and the
     whole group names the record keeper. The founding list and the record keeper have
     not been named yet.
  5. Only what has an outside record can be checked. Three of febu's eleven problems have
     none. That puts them outside this list. It does not make them less important.
  6. Nobody is paid to check. In a simulation, 0 of 400 random pay rules survived every kind
     of cheat. A search found some that did, and the first hole an attacker found in those
     was claims that cite no outside record. That work is not finished. A simulation sorts
     designs. It does not show that one works.
  7. Z wrote the rules, the first claims and the tools, and changed the rules alone three
     times on 10-13. From the first closed claim on, a rule change waits 7 days in the
     chat and goes to the whole group if anyone objects. Until then these are Z's rules.
     The process is not real until a claim closes without Z in any seat.
  8. This is a jury, not a protocol. Bitcoin checks arithmetic. Most of what is here needs a
     person's judgement.
  9. The rules have grown from two pages to about seven. Whoever understands a long
     rulebook holds power over those who do not.
