HOW IT WORKS (the ledger in plain words, written 2026-10-14 by Z, checked by nobody yet)

ONE SENTENCE
  A claim costs a link and a line saying what would prove it wrong; a check costs a click by two
  people who did not write it; the result goes in one text file that anyone can copy and nobody
  can quietly edit.

THE PICTURE
  One entry, four steps, on top. Febu's pages below, in the same four slots. The third slot is the
  one febu never ran. Everything in the picture that is not slot 3 exists to keep slot 3 honest.

HOW IT CONNECTS
  Five places, left to right. The record outside the group is what a claim is about: a
  transaction, an archived page, a dated file. A writer turns it into an entry and posts it in
  the chat. Two members who did not write it open its link, or run its script and open one
  link by hand, and post what they saw. Anyone votes on a rule change. Once a week one member
  copies the chat's posts into ledger.txt and posts the file's hash outside the chat. Two
  matching results close an entry; the file counts them, and that count is PROMISE 7. The
  site is built from the file and adds nothing. The only way into the file is the weekly
  turn; the only way an entry closes is two SEEN lines; the only thing that counts is what
  the file shows.

HOW IT ANSWERS FEBU
  Febu's pages name eleven problems: corrupt leaders, invented debt, repeating history, arrogance,
  war, dependence on machines, machines that imitate, hidden systems, hollow law, misinformation,
  the environment. Read together they share one disease: speech that costs nothing. In every case
  the sentence was free to say and nobody had to check it.

  Febu is the purest case of its own disease. 164 pages, every one about what is wrong, none about
  how anything should work, none with a claim you could test. Written by a script, on a schedule,
  numbered without a gap, so nothing was ever refused. The one checker the story had, UBEF, was
  "removed", in the first person. "Every page survives an argument" was said five minutes after
  being asked who UBEF was. Then the writing stopped (09-19) and the bag was sold (09-22).

  So the ledger is not a fan project and not a treasury. It is the one step febu's pipeline never
  ran: the check.

THE FOUR PARTS
  1. The file. ledger.txt: FACT and PROMISE entries, eight rules, KNOWN BUGS, RISKS, CHANGES.
     Served byte for byte. The pages are built from it and add nothing.
  2. The people. Seven named members, no accounts, no scores. Two close an entry, two keep it
     open, four say yes to a rule change, one per week adds the week's posts and publishes the
     hash.
  3. The outside record. Four things the group does not control: the GitHub history, the hash
     posted outside the chat, a daily Internet Archive capture, and an OpenTimestamps proof in a
     Bitcoin block. None of them stop an edit. Together they make every edit visible, with a date
     nobody in the group set.
  4. The bond. Optional, per promise, in $FEBU. BROKEN means the promiser sends it to the
     incinerator and the transaction is the link. No new promise while a bond is owed. The group
     never holds it.

FOUR QUESTIONS FROM THE CHAT
  WitnesstoFebu read it as: a claim with a link, a WRONG IF and a bond; a cheap check; a result
  set by what the link shows; a record that builds up. Right, except the bond: only a promise
  can carry one. A fact never does. A wrong fact costs its writer the DOESN'T HOLD line.

  1. How big is a bond? The promiser picks it, in $FEBU, with no minimum. A reader weighs the
     promise by it: a promise backed by 10 $FEBU says less than one backed by a million. The
     group never says what that is worth in money (rule 5).
  2. Does a smart contract burn it? No. Within 7 days of a promise closing BROKEN, the promiser
     sends the bond to the incinerator, or burns it, and the transaction is the link. A contract
     holding bonds in advance would be a pool, which rule 5 forbids, and code someone has to
     write and run, which rule 8 calls a bug. Unpaid after 7 days, the entry says UNPAID, and
     its owner posts no new promise until it is paid. That is all the enforcement there is.
  3. Why take part? Nobody is paid, and reputation here is a list, not a score: nothing is
     summed. A writer gets a fact anyone can cite without trusting them, once two others close
     it. A promiser gets believed: a promise with a date and a bond counts for more than a chat
     message. A checker gets something to rely on without taking anyone's word. If seven people
     will not check unpaid, PROMISE 7 breaks, and the dry run's minutes say what pay would cost.
  4. Can I enter "the sun will rise tomorrow"? No. It is a forecast of something nobody here
     does, so it is neither a fact (true now, and a link shows it) nor a promise (something you
     will do). An easy fact that fits ("febu's site is up") can be entered: nobody approves or
     refuses anything. It costs two checkers a minute and earns nothing, because nothing is
     added up. Usefulness is decided by whether anyone bothers to check: an entry nobody needs
     stays OPEN. An easy entry could still count toward PROMISE 7 (RISKS WE ACCEPT).

WHAT IT RUNS ON
  GitHub for the history, Vercel for the pages (a static build and one daily call to the Internet
  Archive), a public Solana RPC and explorer links for chain facts, web.archive.org, the
  OpenTimestamps calendars and Bitcoin for the stamps, and the group chat for posts. It costs
  nothing. If Vercel or GitHub vanish, the file survives in every archive and every copy. If the
  Internet Archive vanishes, the Bitcoin proof survives. If the chat vanishes, the posts move; the
  ledger does not.

  Bitcoin is used for one thing: a timestamp no member could have forged. Not for money, not for
  agreement, not for identity. That is the whole lean.

WHAT IT CANNOT DO
  - Hold a claim with no public record. "Febu is honest" cannot be an entry. "The fee account paid
    nothing out since 09-14" can.
  - Settle what a link means. It settles what a link shows. Interpretation stays in the chat.
  - Work among strangers. It cannot tell one person from two names, or a real check from a copied
    one beyond the "one new thing" rule.
  - Pay anyone or make anyone look. If the seven do not check, PROMISE 7 breaks, and that is the
    honest result. The week-one dry run exists to measure this before anyone argues about pay.
  - Stop an edit. Anyone with the repository can edit; the edit cannot be hidden. The archive and
    the stamps prove what the file was, not that the file was right.
  - Bind anyone. No entity, no contract, no legal weight. A bond is kept by the rule and by shame,
    not by code.
  - Detect a machine. A check written by a model and posted by a person is undetectable; the AI
    line is a rule, not a filter.
  - Show that anyone outside the seven wants it. Seven people and 30 days will say.

WHAT IT COULD BECOME
  - The one record of what febu said and did, with dates, that a holder can check without trusting
    the Assembly.
  - A template. The file and the rules copy in a minute; any group with claims can run its own.
  - $FEBU's first use that is not trading: a bond behind a promise. A broken promise's bond
    goes where nobody can spend it, and the group holds nothing.
  - If febu returns, every new page gets a day-one entry: what it claims, what would show it wrong.
  - Prediction records without a scoreboard: KEPT and BROKEN lines with links, nothing summed.
  - The first real numbers on what a check costs in minutes, which every later decision about pay
    needs.
