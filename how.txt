HOW IT WORKS (the ledger in plain words, written 2026-10-14 by Z, revised for version 5, checked by nobody yet)
ONE SENTENCE
  An answer costs a link and a line saying what would prove it wrong; a close costs a look by
  three people who did not post it, one of them by hand; the result goes in one text file that
  anyone can copy and nobody can quietly edit.

WHERE IT STANDS
  Version 5 of the ledger replaced version 3 on the day it was adopted: STATUS and CHANGES TO THE
  RULES in ledger.txt give the date. On that day nothing had been checked. The group works on one
  question at a time. A proposal opens as the next question once three members other than its
  proposer have written "yes"; proposals posted before adoption stand, and their yeses count
  from adoption. Version 3's nine entries are kept word for word in
  archive/08_CLAIMS_LEDGER_v3.txt. Any member may propose one as a question, and its wording may
  be posted as an answer, with its version-3 writer among the answer's posters.

THE PICTURE
  One answer, four steps, on top: written, checked, a result, a record. Febu's pages below, in
  the same four slots. The third slot is the one febu never ran. Everything in the picture that
  is not that slot exists to keep it honest. The lower row is Z's reading of febu's pages and
  tweets, not answers. The picture was drawn for version 3: where it says entry, read answer;
  two checkers are now three, one of them by hand; its promise, its bond and PROMISE 7 are gone
  (rule 9, WHEN THIS HAS FAILED).

HOW IT CONNECTS
  Five places, left to right. The record outside the group is what a question is about: a
  transaction, an archived page, a dated file. A member proposes a question in the chat, and
  the first proposal to get three yeses from other members opens; it is the only open question
  until it closes. Anyone posts an answer to it in the chat. Members who did not post it open
  its links and post what they saw; a script none of its posters wrote may count, with one link
  opened by hand (Saw). Three matching results close an answer, and at least one of the three
  opened every link by hand. An answer that closes HOLDS closes the question, and the next
  proposal opens. Members vote on a rule change in the chat. Once a week one member makes sure
  every result, proposal, yes and vote posted in the chat is in the file and every one in the
  file has its chat post, adds a LAST POSTS line, then posts the file's hash in the chat and in
  one named public place. Anyone may add to the file through the chat and the weekly turn. The
  site is built from the texts and adds no rule: where it and ledger.txt differ, ledger.txt
  wins. The picture was drawn for version 3: its ENTRY post is now an ANSWER post, a PROPOSE or
  YES post opens a question, a close takes three checks, not two, and PROMISE 7 is now WHEN
  THIS HAS FAILED.

THE FOUR PARTS
  1. The loop (rules 1 to 5). One question at a time, about febu or something febu touches,
     asking what a link anyone can open could settle. Any member proposes one in the chat;
     three yeses from other members open it, and the first proposal to reach three opens next.
     Nobody judges whether a question is worth asking. Anyone posts an answer to the open
     question: what it SAYS, LINKs that open for anyone, and a WRONG IF line for every part of
     SAYS, fixed before any check. A WRONG IF line about something that can still change ends
     on a stated date. The answer is the group's: the pages show it as the Assembly's, and the
     file keeps who posted it so that rule 4 can be checked. Three members who did not post it
     each open the links and post what they saw, which lines they tested, and one thing on the
     page nobody had written; at least one of them opens every link by hand, with no script. A
     post that only says HOLDS counts for nothing. Three matching results close it HOLDS or
     DOESN'T HOLD. HOLDS closes the question; DOESN'T HOLD stays, marked, and the question waits
     for another answer. A line over many items (pages, payments) counts toward HOLDS only if
     tested over all of them. Two people, member or not, against two others keep an answer
     open; two who post the result it did not close on reopen it (a non-member has nowhere to
     post it yet: KNOWN BUGS 7). A question not closed in 30 days is CAN'T TELL, and the next
     one opens. Anyone may change an answer with a dated CHANGED line until its first SEEN item,
     never after. Nobody approves anything and nobody checks an answer they posted. An AI may
     help someone look; it never posts a result.

  2. The keeping (rules 6 and 7). One file, ledger.txt. Nothing is removed or rewritten: every
     change is a new dated line. Each week the next member in the list checks both ways, adds
     what is missing and one LAST POSTS line, and posts the new file's SHA-256 in the chat and
     in one named public place outside it. The ledger is whichever copy matches the last posted
     hash. Three records sit outside the group: that posted hash, a daily request to the
     Internet Archive to save ledger.txt, and a Bitcoin timestamp (OpenTimestamps), so far of
     version 3's last text, in Bitcoin block 971607. GitHub holds the history too, but the
     repository is Z's (KNOWN BUGS 1) and its history was cut once, on 2026-10-15, when the
     ledger started clean. None of these stops an edit. Together they make an edit visible,
     with a date no member set.

  3. The changing (rules 8 and 10). A rule changes, and anything is said or done in the group's
     name beyond opening questions and closing answers, only after 7 days in the chat, four
     yeses from active members (every active member, if fewer than five are active, and never
     fewer than two) and at most one "object". An objection says which line is wrong and what
     would change the objector's mind. The votes are counted from the file's VOTES section, and
     the old wording stays, word for word, in archive/. Anything only one person can do is a
     bug, listed under KNOWN BUGS with a date to fix it by. Today that includes: Z holds the
     file and the site, and Z wrote every script. A member counts as active while the file
     shows a post of theirs from the last 90 days.

  4. The test (WHEN THIS HAS FAILED). Read at the first weekly turn after the 60th day from
     adoption, unpaid, on the seven. If by then fewer than three questions have closed under
     rule 4 (CAN'T TELL does not count), or one person posted every answer that closed HOLDS,
     it has failed: we say so in the chat, and rewrite it or stop. No second vote is needed.
     It replaces version 3's PROMISE 7 and its stop rule, E0 to E3: the one check by hand in
     every close does the work the script tests were for.

  Money is not a part. The group holds none and moves none in its name: no pool, no treasury,
  no bonds, no pay, no new token (rule 9). $FEBU is the only token named, and nothing in the
  group's name states or implies a price for it. No totals, scores or rankings of members, in
  the file or on the group's pages.

WHAT IT RUNS ON
  GitHub for the history (Z's repository), Vercel for the pages and two small daily jobs (the
  Internet Archive save, and a read that keeps the den's free database awake), a public Solana
  RPC and explorer links for chain facts, web.archive.org for the daily capture, and the group
  chat for posts. It runs on free tiers. If Vercel or GitHub vanish, the file survives in every
  copy anyone made. If the Internet Archive vanishes, a Bitcoin timestamp still shows the file
  existed (timestamps/ holds one, for version 3's last text). If the chat vanishes, the posts
  move; the ledger does not.

  Bitcoin is used for one thing: a timestamp no member could have forged. Not for money, not
  for agreement, not for identity. That is the whole lean.

WHAT IT CANNOT DO
  - Ask a question with no public record. "Is febu honest?" cannot be a question. "Did the fee
    account pay anything out from 09-15 to the end of 10-14?" can. An answer about something a
    stranger could change (a dust transfer, the fee claim anyone may send) needs a WRONG IF
    line that ends on a stated date (rule 3).
  - Settle what a link means. It settles what a link shows. Interpretation stays in the chat.
  - Work among strangers. It cannot tell one person from two names, or a real check from a
    copied one beyond the "one new thing" rule and the one check by hand.
  - Make anyone look. The group pays nobody. A question nobody works on holds the slot for its
    30 days. If the seven do not check, WHEN THIS HAS FAILED says so, and that is the honest
    result.
  - Stop an edit. Anyone with the repository can edit; the edit cannot be hidden. A capture and
    a stamp prove what the file was, not that the file was right.
  - Bind anyone. No entity, no contract, no legal weight. The rules are kept by the record and
    by shame, not by code.
  - Detect a machine. A check written by a model and posted by a person is undetectable; the AI
    line is a rule, not a filter.
  - Keep out easy questions. One anyone could answer without looking can be proposed, answered
    and closed. It counts toward WHEN THIS HAS FAILED and shows nothing: three yeses are the
    only gate.
  - Show that anyone outside the seven wants it. Seven people and 60 days will say about the
    seven, not about outsiders.

WHAT IT IS WORTH
  The group does not decide what anything is worth. It makes one thing cheap, finding out
  whether an answer is true, and leaves the price to whoever wants the answer. Bitcoin works the
  same way: its rules fix what a check costs, and nothing in them says what the coin is worth.
  Nobody is paid for a check, a script or anything else (rule 9). Version 3's shape for a member
  who pays for scripts (paid.md) is not part of version 5; bringing pay back is a rule change
  (rule 8).

HOW IT ANSWERS FEBU
  This section is Z's reading of febu's pages and tweets. None of it is a question yet. The
  09-22 sale was version 3's fact 2.

  Febu's pages name eleven problems: corrupt leaders, invented debt, repeating history, arrogance,
  war, dependence on machines, machines that imitate, hidden systems, hollow law, misinformation,
  the environment. Read together they share one disease: speech that costs nothing. In every case
  the sentence was free to say and nobody had to check it.

  Febu is the purest case of its own disease. 164 pages, every one about what is wrong, none about
  how anything should work, none with a claim you could test. Written by a script, on a schedule,
  numbered without a gap: no sign anything was ever refused. The one checker the story had, UBEF,
  was "removed", in the first person. "Every page survives an argument" was said five minutes
  after being asked who UBEF was. Then the writing stopped (09-19) and the bag was sold (09-22).

  So the ledger is not a fan project and not a treasury. It is the one step febu's pipeline never
  ran: the check.

FOUR QUESTIONS FROM THE CHAT
  WitnesstoFebu read version 3 as: a claim with a link, a WRONG IF and a bond; a cheap check; a
  result set by what the link shows; a record that builds up. Version 5 keeps all of it but the
  bond. A wrong answer costs nothing but its DOESN'T HOLD line, and that line stays.

  1. How big is a bond? There are none now (rule 9). Version 3 let a promise name one in $FEBU;
     version 5 has no promises and moves no money in the group's name.
  2. Does a smart contract burn it? There is nothing to burn. A contract holding money would be
     a pool, which rule 9 forbids, and code someone has to write and run is a step only that
     person can do, which rule 10 lists as a bug.
  3. Why take part? The group pays nobody, and nothing is summed: no totals, scores or rankings
     (rule 9). You get an answer anyone can cite without trusting whoever posted it, once three
     others close it, and something to rely on without taking anyone's word. If seven people
     will not check unpaid, WHEN THIS HAS FAILED says so at day 60.
  4. Can I ask "will the sun rise tomorrow"? No. A question asks what a link anyone can open
     could settle; a forecast of what nobody here does goes in the chat (rule 1). An easy
     question that fits ("is febu's site up?") can be proposed: nobody judges whether a question
     is worth asking, and three yeses are the only gate. It costs the group a slot and earns
     nothing, because nothing is added up. It could still count toward WHEN THIS HAS FAILED
     (RISKS WE ACCEPT).

WHAT IT COULD BECOME
  - A dated record of what febu said and did, built one question at a time, that a holder can
    check without trusting the Assembly.
  - A template. The file and the rules copy in a minute; any small group whose members know each
    other can run its own.
  - If febu returns, a member can propose a question about each new page: what it claims, and
    what would show it wrong.
  - A record without a scoreboard: HOLDS and DOESN'T HOLD lines with links, nothing summed.
  - The first real numbers on how long a question takes to close, from the first ones.
