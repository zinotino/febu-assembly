HOW IT WORKS (the ledger in plain words, written 2026-10-14 by Z, revised 2026-10-15, checked by nobody yet)
ONE SENTENCE
  A claim costs a link and a line saying what would prove it wrong; a check costs a look by two
  people who did not write it; the result goes in one text file that anyone can copy and nobody
  can quietly edit.

WHERE IT STANDS
  Version 3 of the ledger is proposed, not adopted, and nobody but its writer has checked anything
  in it. Until 2026-10-22 the seven members post "yes" or "object" in the chat on three things:
  the ledger as served, the Saw line (running a script the entry's writer did not write, then
  opening one link by hand, may count as looking), and the stop rule (the 30-day test, E0 to
  E3). The same week is a dry run: facts 1, 3 and 9 are the cheapest to check, and a post says
  how many minutes it took. Posts made before adoption close nothing and do not count toward
  PROMISE 7. Until the vote closes, ledger.txt and process.md do not change. The 30 days start
  at adoption.

THE PICTURE
  One entry, four steps, on top: written, checked, a result, a record. Febu's pages below, in the
  same four slots. The third slot is the one febu never ran. Everything in the picture that is not
  that slot exists to keep it honest. The lower row is Z's reading of febu's pages and tweets, not
  entries; the one entry in it is the 09-22 sale (FACT 2), and nobody has checked it.

HOW IT CONNECTS
  Five places, left to right. The record outside the group is what a claim is about: a
  transaction, an archived page, a dated file. A writer turns it into an entry and posts it in the
  chat. Two members who did not write it open its link and post what they saw; if the Saw line
  passes, they may instead run a script its writer did not write and open one link by hand. Members
  vote on a rule change. Once a week one member makes sure every result posted in the chat is in
  the file and every SEEN line has its chat post, then posts the file's hash in the chat and in one
  named public place. Two matching results close an entry. For PROMISE 7 a close counts on the
  day of its second post; the file catches up at the next weekly turn. Anyone may add a SEEN line
  to the file. The site is built from the texts and adds no rule: where it and ledger.txt differ,
  ledger.txt wins.

THE FOUR PARTS
  1. The loop (rules 1 to 4). An entry is a FACT or a PROMISE. A fact says what is true now,
     gives a link anyone can open, and carries a WRONG IF line, fixed before any check, saying
     what would show it false. A promise says what its writer will do, by when, and the link
     anyone will open then; it may name a bond. Two members who did not write the entry open the
     link and post what they saw and one thing on the page nobody had written; a post that only
     says HOLDS counts for nothing. The same result twice closes it. One person's disagreement is
     a note under it. Two people, member or not, who post the same different result reopen it
     (a non-member has nowhere to post it yet: KNOWN BUGS 7). 30 days without a close is CAN'T
     TELL. A line over many items (pages, payments) counts toward HOLDS only if tested over all
     of them. The writer may correct an entry with a dated CHANGED line until its first SEEN
     line, never after. Nobody approves an entry and nobody checks their own. An AI may help
     someone look; it never posts a result.

  2. The keeping (rule 6). One file, ledger.txt. Each week the next member in the list checks
     both ways, adds what is missing, and posts the new file's SHA-256 in the chat and in one
     named public place outside it. The ledger is whichever copy matches the last posted hash.
     Three records sit outside the group: that posted hash, a daily request to the Internet
     Archive to save ledger.txt, and a Bitcoin timestamp (OpenTimestamps) of the current
     version, in Bitcoin block 971607. GitHub holds the history too, but the repository is Z's
     (KNOWN BUGS 1) and its history was cut once, on 2026-10-15, when the ledger started clean.
     None of these stops an edit. Together they make an edit visible, with a date no member set.

  3. The changing (rules 7 and 8). A rule changes, and anything is done in the group's name,
     only after 7 days in the chat, four yeses from active members (every active member, if fewer
     than five are active) and at most one "object". The old wording stays. Anything only one
     person can do is a bug, listed under KNOWN BUGS with a date to fix it by. Today that
     includes: Z holds the file and the site. A member silent for 90 days stops counting until
     they post again.

  4. The test (PROMISE 7 and the stop rule). Thirty days after adoption, unpaid, on the seven. The
     group's goal, PROMISE 7: close three entries besides itself, at least one not written by Z.
     Miss it and it is BROKEN: we say so, then rewrite or stop. The stop rule adds four tests, E0
     to E3, each with its failing number written first and what happens when it fails. The
     first honest numbers on what a check costs in minutes come out of this part.

  Money is not a part. The group holds none: no pool, no treasury, no new token. A promise may
  name a bond in $FEBU; if it ends BROKEN its writer sends the bond to the incinerator and the
  transaction is the link. A fact never carries a bond. The group pays nobody; a member may pay
  another only by their own promise.

WHAT IT RUNS ON
  GitHub for the history (Z's repository), Vercel for the pages and two small daily jobs (the
  Internet Archive save, and a read that keeps the den's free database awake), a public Solana
  RPC and explorer links for chain facts, web.archive.org for the daily capture, and the group
  chat for posts. It runs on free tiers. If Vercel or GitHub vanish, the file survives in every
  copy anyone made. If the Internet Archive vanishes, a Bitcoin timestamp still shows the file
  existed (timestamps/ holds one for the current version). If the chat vanishes, the posts move;
  the ledger does not.

  Bitcoin is used for one thing: a timestamp no member could have forged. Not for money, not
  for agreement, not for identity. That is the whole lean.

WHAT IT CANNOT DO
  - Hold a claim with no public record. "Febu is honest" cannot be an entry. "The fee account
    paid nothing out from 09-15 to the end of 10-14" can. A claim a stranger could make false
    (a dust transfer, the fee claim anyone may send) needs an end date; facts 2, 6 and 8 were
    changed for that.
  - Settle what a link means. It settles what a link shows. Interpretation stays in the chat.
  - Work among strangers. It cannot tell one person from two names, or a real check from a copied
    one beyond the "one new thing" rule.
  - Make anyone look. The group pays nobody. If the seven do not check, PROMISE 7 breaks, and
    that is the honest result. The dry run's minutes will say what a check costs.
  - Stop an edit. Anyone with the repository can edit; the edit cannot be hidden. A capture and a
    stamp prove what the file was, not that the file was right.
  - Bind anyone. No entity, no contract, no legal weight. A bond is kept by the rule and by shame,
    not by code.
  - Detect a machine. A check written by a model and posted by a person is undetectable; the AI
    line is a rule, not a filter.
  - Show that anyone outside the seven wants it. Seven people and 30 days will say about the
    seven, not about outsiders.

FOUR QUESTIONS FROM THE CHAT
  WitnesstoFebu read it as: a claim with a link, a WRONG IF and a bond; a cheap check; a result
  set by what the link shows; a record that builds up. Right, except the bond: only a promise
  can carry one. A fact never does. A wrong fact costs its writer the DOESN'T HOLD line.

  1. How big is a bond? The promiser picks it, in $FEBU, with no minimum. A reader weighs the
     promise by it: a promise backed by 10 $FEBU says less than one backed by a million. The
     group never says what that is worth in money (rule 5).
  2. Does a smart contract burn it? No. Within 7 days of a promise closing BROKEN, the promiser
     sends the bond to the incinerator, or burns it, and the transaction is the link. A contract
     holding bonds in advance would be a pool, which rule 5 forbids, and code someone has to
     write and run is a step only that person can do, which rule 8 lists as a bug. Unpaid after
     7 days, the entry says UNPAID, and its owner posts no new promise until it is paid. That is
     all the enforcement there is.
  3. Why take part? The group pays nobody, and reputation here is a list, not a score: nothing
     is summed. A writer gets a fact anyone can cite without trusting them, once two others close
     it. A promiser gets believed: a promise with a date and a bond counts for more than a chat
     message. A checker gets something to rely on without taking anyone's word. If seven people
     will not check unpaid, PROMISE 7 breaks, and the dry run's minutes say what a check costs.
     What anything is worth, and who would be paid later, is the next section.
  4. Can I enter "the sun will rise tomorrow"? No. It is a forecast of something nobody here
     does, so it is neither a fact (true now, and a link shows it) nor a promise (something you
     will do). An easy fact that fits ("febu's site is up") can be entered: nobody approves or
     refuses anything. It costs two checkers a minute and earns nothing, because nothing is
     added up. Usefulness is decided by whether anyone bothers to check: an entry nobody needs
     stays OPEN. An easy entry could still count toward PROMISE 7 (RISKS WE ACCEPT).

WHAT IT IS WORTH, AND WHO WOULD BE PAID
  The group does not decide what anything is worth. It makes one thing cheap, finding out whether
  a claim is true, and leaves the price to whoever wants the answer. Bitcoin works the same way:
  its rules fix what a check costs, and nothing in them says what the coin is worth.
  - A check. What it costs in minutes is measured in the first weeks (process.md, section 7).
    That is the floor for any pay. Below it nobody checks; far above it people check things that
    cannot be false.
  - A task. Someone who wants it done posts their own promise, with a bond, naming what they will
    pay for a script that two others reproduce (paid.md). Anyone may post a competing promise.
    If nobody takes the work, it was worth less than that to everyone else. The group holds no
    money and sets no price.
  - Creative work, a book or a video. The ledger cannot judge it. It can show that it was
    published, when, and that its author did what they said they would. Its worth is for the
    audience to say.
  Until day 30 nobody is paid: you get a record anyone can cite, and a promise that counts for
  more because of its bond. After day 30, if the 30-day test says keep, pay opens for scripts
  only, and two tests say whether pay changes anything and whether anyone besides the funder
  wants to pay. The loop where it pays for itself closes only if people other than the funder
  post bounties. Nobody knows that yet.

WHAT IT COULD BECOME
  - A dated record of what febu said and did, built from entries, that a holder can check
    without trusting the Assembly.
  - A template. The file and the rules copy in a minute; any small group whose members know each
    other can run its own.
  - A bond behind a promise, in $FEBU: a use that is not trading. Buying $FEBU for one still pays
    its fee to the wallet that created it (KNOWN BUGS 5).
  - If febu returns, a member can give each new page a day-one entry: what it claims, what would
    show it wrong.
  - Promise records without a scoreboard: KEPT and BROKEN lines with links, nothing summed.
  - The first real numbers on what a check costs in minutes, from the cheapest entries first.
